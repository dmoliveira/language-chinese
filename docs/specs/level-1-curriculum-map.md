# Level 1 Curriculum Map

## Objective

Turn Level 1 from a broad label into the first true handbook section after the foundations.

## Level 1 promise

By the end of Level 1, a learner should be able to handle simple daily-life Chinese in short, supported interactions and texts.

## Suggested scope

- routine and schedule
- food and basic preferences
- family and people
- places and movement
- study and work basics
- simple plans and likes/dislikes

## Proposed structure

### Stage 1 — Daily Routine and Time

- listening: simple daily routine listening
- reading: simple daily routine reading
- writing: write your basic daily routine
- speaking: talk about your daily routine
- references: time expressions, routine verbs, question forms
- deck: daily routine core set
- checkpoint: describe your day in 4 to 6 lines / sentences

### Stage 2 — Food, Needs, and Preferences

- food and drink vocabulary
- ordering or choosing basic items
- likes/dislikes patterns
- simple restaurant or meal dialogue

### Stage 3 — Family, People, and Personal Information

- family vocabulary
- describing people simply
- age, job, study, language ability

### Stage 4 — Places, Movement, and Basic Plans

- here/there/go/come
- time/place combinations
- simple plans and scheduling

## Required assets per stage

- 4 core lessons (listening, reading, writing, speaking)
- at least 2 reference pages
- 1 deck
- 1 stage checkpoint
- review prompts

## Assessment expectations

- lesson checks throughout
- stage-end integrated task
- visible can-do statements

## Tech overlay entry point

After Stage 2 or Stage 3, introduce an optional beginner-friendly technical overlay:

- study routine for coding/learning
- simple work/study self-introductions
- device/software vocabulary basics

## Delivery rule

Build Level 1 by stages, not by isolated lesson types. Each stage should feel complete enough for a learner to follow end to end.
