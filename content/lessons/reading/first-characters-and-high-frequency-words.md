# First Characters and High-Frequency Words

## Objectives

- Recognize a small set of common beginner characters.
- Match hanzi with pinyin and English meaning.
- Read a very short beginner text with support.

## Prerequisites

- No prior reading knowledge required.
- Helpful: basic pinyin familiarity.

## Vocabulary and Patterns

- 我 — wǒ — I, me
- 你 — nǐ — you
- 好 — hǎo — good
- 叫 — jiào — to be called
- 名字 — míngzi — name

Short combinations:

- 你好 — nǐ hǎo — hello
- 我叫 ... — wǒ jiào ... — my name is ...

Recognition goal:

- Notice that even a very short text becomes easier when you can identify a few repeated characters.

## Before You Read

Before reading, scan the text and try to spot:

- 我
- 你
- 叫
- 名字

Do not try to read perfectly on the first pass.
First look for familiar shapes and repeated words.

## Lesson Content

Read this short text three times:

> 你好！(nǐ hǎo) — Hello!
>
> 我叫 Lin。(wǒ jiào Lin.) — My name is Lin.
>
> 你叫什么名字？(nǐ jiào shénme míngzi?) — What is your name?

Reading support:

- 你好 = hello
- 我叫 Lin = My name is Lin
- 你叫什么名字？= What is your name?

What to notice:

- **我** appears in self-introductions.
- **你** appears when speaking directly to another person.
- **叫** is useful very early because it helps you say your name.

Reading strategy:

- First pass: look for familiar words.
- Second pass: connect pinyin and hanzi.
- Third pass: read for full meaning.

Mini breakdown:

- 你 + 叫 + 什么 + 名字？
- Literally: you + are called + what + name?
- Natural English: What is your name?

## Character Attention

Spend one minute on each of these:

- **我**: often appears in beginner self-introduction sentences
- **你**: often appears in questions or direct address
- **好**: appears in greetings and positive descriptions
- **叫**: appears when talking about names

Ask yourself:

- Can I visually recognize the character?
- Can I connect it with sound?
- Can I connect it with meaning?

## Practice

1. Which character means “I”?
2. Which character means “you”?
3. Find the phrase that asks for a name.
4. Read the short text aloud once with pinyin support.
5. Which character appears in both **你好** and **我很好**?
6. Which word in the text is a question about identity?
7. Read the text once without English support.
8. Underline the self-introduction part of the text.

## Answer Key or Rubric

1. **我**
2. **你**
3. **你叫什么名字？**
4. Successful if the learner can roughly map each line to meaning and pronunciation.
5. **好**
6. **名字** appears in the question asking for a name.
7. Success if the learner can read with partial support and keep the overall meaning.
8. **我叫 Lin。**

## Extension Practice

If this lesson feels manageable, try:

- cover the English and read only hanzi + pinyin
- cover the pinyin and see what you still recognize
- replace **Lin** with your own name
- copy the text once by hand for recognition practice

## Reading Confidence Notes

At this level, progress means:

- you recognize more than before
- you are less afraid of short Chinese text
- you can connect a few characters with sound and meaning
