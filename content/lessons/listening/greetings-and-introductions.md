# Greetings and Introductions

## Objectives

- Recognize a few common greetings in pinyin and hanzi.
- Understand a short self-introduction.
- Reply with a simple greeting and name.

## Prerequisites

- No prior Chinese required.
- Review `reference-foundations-001` if pinyin feels unfamiliar.

## Vocabulary and Patterns

- 你好 — nǐ hǎo — hello
- 你好吗？— nǐ hǎo ma? — how are you?
- 我很好 — wǒ hěn hǎo — I am very well
- 我叫 Anna — wǒ jiào Anna — my name is Anna
- 很高兴认识你 — hěn gāoxìng rènshi nǐ — nice to meet you

Pattern:

- 我叫 + name

Useful response chunks:

- 你好！— hello!
- 你呢？— and you?
- 很高兴认识你。— nice to meet you.

## Before You Listen

Before you listen, look at these ideas:

- You do not need to understand every word.
- Your first goal is to catch the topic: greeting, name, and polite closing.
- Your second goal is to hear repeated chunks.

Good chunks to listen for:

- 你好
- 我叫
- 你呢
- 很高兴认识你

## Lesson Content

Listen for the overall meaning first. Do not worry if you cannot catch every syllable on the first try.

Sample dialogue:

1. A: 你好！(nǐ hǎo) — Hello!
2. B: 你好！(nǐ hǎo) — Hello!
3. A: 你叫什么名字？(nǐ jiào shénme míngzi?) — What is your name?
4. B: 我叫 Anna。你呢？(wǒ jiào Anna. nǐ ne?) — My name is Anna. And you?
5. A: 我叫 David。很高兴认识你。(wǒ jiào David. hěn gāoxìng rènshi nǐ.) — My name is David. Nice to meet you.
6. B: 很高兴认识你。(hěn gāoxìng rènshi nǐ.) — Nice to meet you.

Listening tips:

- Notice that **nǐ hǎo** is short and clear.
- Listen for **wǒ jiào** to catch a name introduction.
- Listen for **nǐ ne** to hear “and you?”

Second-pass listening focus:

- Which speaker introduces their name first?
- Where does the conversation switch from question to answer?
- Which line closes the meeting politely?

Meaning check:

- This dialogue is not a full formal introduction.
- It is a short, useful beginner exchange.
- The main purpose is to help you hear greeting rhythm and self-introduction structure.

## Shadowing Practice

Try shadowing, which means repeating just after the line finishes.

Round 1:

- Repeat only **你好** and **我叫 ...**

Round 2:

- Repeat full short lines

Round 3:

- Say the whole dialogue with pauses

Shadowing checklist:

- keep phrases short
- copy the rhythm, not only the words
- do not rush the tones
- pause briefly after each sentence

## Practice

1. What phrase means “my name is ...”?
2. In the sample dialogue, what are the two names?
3. Which line means “nice to meet you”?
4. Say your own name after: **我叫 ...**
5. Which phrase means “and you?”
6. Listen again and count how many times **你好** appears.
7. Replace **Anna** with your own name and say the line aloud.
8. Act out the dialogue with a partner or by speaking both roles.

## Answer Key or Rubric

1. **我叫 ...**
2. **Anna** and **David**
3. **很高兴认识你**
4. Learner should produce a simple self-introduction with a name.
5. **你呢？**
6. **Two times**
7. Any clear self-introduction using **我叫 ...** is acceptable.
8. Success if the learner keeps the basic order and meaning of the exchange.

Rubric for open speaking practice:

- recognizes greeting clearly
- can produce a name introduction
- can respond politely at the end

## Extension Practice

If this lesson feels comfortable, try these extensions:

- Change both names in the dialogue.
- Say the dialogue faster but keep the words clear.
- Cover the English meaning and use only hanzi or pinyin.
- Listen once, then summarize the dialogue in English.
